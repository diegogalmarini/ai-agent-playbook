# LinkedIn weekly

Drafts next week's LinkedIn content for one person. The person reviews and publishes. This skill never publishes, schedules or comments on its own.

## Inputs

- A folder of real material: notes, project logs, lessons learned, metrics (a "second brain", an Obsidian vault, a docs folder).
- A short style guide in the person's words (`how-i-write.md`).
- A list of what must never appear in public (clients without permission, confidential projects, employers in dispute).

## Hard rules

- Never invent a number, a client or a story. Each post cites the note it comes from. Missing facts are written `TO CONFIRM`.
- Never publish. Output is a file for the person to review.
- Nothing from the never-public list, however well it would perform.

## What works on LinkedIn in 2026 (sources at the end)

- Documents and carousels get the highest engagement rate; multi-image next; plain links the lowest.
- Text with a visual beats a post with an outbound link. Links go in the first comment.
- Consistency in one niche and meaningful comments matter more than volume. 3 to 5 posts a week is enough.

## Steps

1. Read what changed in the material in the last 7 days, plus the style guide.
2. Read previous weekly files so no topic repeats.
3. Pick 3 real topics, one per pillar:
   - **Build**: numbers, costs, errors measured.
   - **Lessons**: a story with a real mistake.
   - **Method**: rules, templates, prompts, skills the reader can copy.
4. Write each post:
   - Hook in the first 210 characters, starting with a number or a fact, never with a question.
   - 900 to 1,300 characters, paragraphs of one or two lines.
   - One real doubt or error.
   - Give something the reader can use today (a template, a prompt, a checklist, a repo).
   - Close with a specific question. At most 2 hashtags. No link in the body.
   - Plain words, no AI filler ("delve", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world").
5. Every post gets a visual: a carousel (PDF, 1080x1080, page counter, one idea per page), an infographic or a real photo. Describe it, or generate it if a tool is available.
6. Translate the strongest post into English for a second day.
7. Add 8 to 10 people or profile types to comment on that week (70% peers, 20% aspirational, 10% potential clients), with a draft comment for each that adds a number, an experience or a precise question.
8. Ask for last week's metrics: impressions, comments, saves, profile visits, new followers.
9. Save everything in one dated file with each post ready to paste, its source and what the person must check.

## Sources

- DSMN8, employee advocacy report (early 2026): text posts averaged 775 impressions vs 149 for posts with a link.
